# Match Cascade

**Smarter matching that feeds the AI, without bypassing human approval.**

---

## 1. The problem

Every payment appears twice: once in **our ledger**, once in the **processor's settlement report**.
Reconciliation is a giant "match the following" question, and it is hard because:

- There is **no shared ID** between the two records.
- Processors take **fees** (amount is 1–5¢ short), **round** differently, and **settle late** (date is a day or two off).
- References get **reformatted** along the way (`PAY-150000` becomes `pay150000` or `P/150000-SETTLED`).

```text
Ledger:      CUST-0247, PROC_C, $155.20, PAY-150000, 26 Sep
Settlement:  CUST-0247, PROC_C, $155.17, pay150000,  27 Sep
             same payment?  probably, but 3¢ short and 1 day late
```

---

## 2. Before: one matcher, all or nothing

The original `match()` pairs records only when the **cleaned reference** is equal and **all seven checks** pass.

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| Exactly one candidate passes | ✅ MATCH |
| A check fails | ❌ BREAK, labelled with the reason |
| More than one candidate | ⚠️ AMBIGUOUS, parked for a human |
| Reference mangled | ❌ NO_CANDIDATE, no partner, no evidence |

The leftover breaks are what the AI learns from. If a break has no partner, the AI **cannot see it at all**.

---

## 3. After: a five-step cascade

Each step only sees what the earlier steps could not handle, and a settlement can never be used twice.

```text
All records
   │
   ▼
① match_by_id            exact reference, all checks pass        → MATCH
   ▼  leftovers
② match_by_rule          cleaned reference, all checks pass      → MATCH
   ▼  leftovers
③ match_by_fuzzy_score   find the likely partner, measure the    → EVIDENCE
   ▼                     difference (case stays open)
④ match_by_llm           AI picks among top 5 candidates         → SUGGESTION
   ▼                     (only rows fuzzy could not pair)
⑤ match_by_human         person accepts or rejects               → MATCH / CONFIRMED BREAK
```

**Fast, trusted steps first; expensive and uncertain steps last.**

---

## 4. The key idea: fuzzy finds the pair, AI explains the change

Fuzzy scoring does **not** resolve records. It says *"A probably goes with B"*, and code measures exactly what differs:

```json
{
  "processor_record_id": "SET-0042",
  "fuzzy_score": 0.94,
  "amount_delta_cents": -3,
  "date_delta_days": 1,
  "reference_raw_equal": false,
  "checks": { "customer": true, "currency": true, "amount_patch": false }
}
```

The AI then explains the **pattern** across many pairs:

> "312 PROC_C pairs are 3¢ short and 1 day late — a fixed processing fee. Propose PROC_C tolerance = 3¢."

That proposal still goes through **backtest → guardrails → human approval** before it becomes a rule.
Once approved, `match_by_rule` catches those cases automatically, and fuzzy moves on to the next new pattern.

---

## 5. Why fuzzy must not auto-match

We tried it first. Fuzzy auto-matching quietly resolved **1,068 fee cases**, and that broke the system's core loop:

```text
Fuzzy silently matches all 3¢ fee cases
   → no AMOUNT_MISMATCH cases left over
   → detect_incidents finds nothing
   → diagnosis and evolution agents never run
   → no rule is ever proposed or approved
```

It also accepted up to 25¢ differences that **no human had approved**, which is exactly what ReconForge exists to prevent.

| | Fuzzy = finished match | Fuzzy = evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Record status | Resolved | Open, with likely partner attached |
| AI sees it | No | Yes |
| Rule learned | Never | Proposed, backtested, approved |
| Human approval | Skipped | Required |

---

## 6. Safety rails built into code

The AI can suggest; code decides what is allowed.

- **One-to-one:** a settlement claimed by two records is dropped from every step.
- **Hard boundaries:** fuzzy and AI candidates must share customer, currency, merchant, and processor.
- **High value:** records of $10,000 or more are never fuzzy-paired.
- **Amount ceiling:** the gap is measured after approved fees, and a gap at the policy's maximum tolerance (25¢) scores zero.
- **Clear winner:** a fuzzy pair needs the policy's minimum similarity (0.95) and minimum lead over the runner-up (0.10).
- **No invented IDs:** an AI suggestion naming a record outside its candidate list is discarded.
- **Confidence floor:** AI suggestions below 0.7 confidence are discarded.
- **Human decisions validated:** a choice of an unknown or already-matched settlement is rejected.

---

## 7. Fuzzy follows the policy

Fuzzy has no private thresholds: every limit comes from the active, human-approved policy.

| Fuzzy needs | Policy setting | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum score to pair | `reference_matching.minimum_similarity` | 0.95 |
| Minimum lead over runner-up | `reference_matching.minimum_candidate_margin` | 0.10 |
| Amount gap that scores zero | `amount_matching.maximum_tolerance_cents` | 25¢ |
| Fees to subtract first | `fees` and processor overrides | none approved yet |
| AI suggestion confidence floor | `escalation.confidence_min` | 0.7 |

```text
Stripe takes an approved 2.9% fee
Ledger $100.00  →  expected after fee $97.10  →  settlement $97.09
Old fuzzy: gap $2.91, score 0.70, not paired
New fuzzy: gap 1¢,    score 0.99, paired and explained
```

Making fuzzy looser or stricter is a **policy change**: proposed, backtested, and approved like any other.

---

## 8. A bug we caught on the way

The first fuzzy version scored amount closeness relative to 1% of the payment. On a $260 payment, a **50¢** gap looked "close".

| Pattern in the data | Amount gap |
|---|---|
| Processing fee | 1–5¢ |
| Rounding difference | 1¢ |
| **Real amount mismatch** | **50¢ – $25** |

Result: **20 wrong pairs**, every one a real mismatch. Capping the gap at 25¢ brought wrong pairs to **zero**.

> Caveat: the 25¢ cap was chosen after inspecting this dataset, so these results are optimistic for unseen data.
> It is justified independently by the existing policy ceiling, not tuned to the numbers.

---

## 9. Results on the demo dataset

Checked against the protected ground truth, which the matching code never reads. Fuzzy uses the baseline policy's thresholds.

| | Historical (8,000) | Live (1,500) |
|---|---|---|
| Auto-matched by id | 6,198 | 1,029 |
| Auto-matched by rule | 460 | 59 |
| **Wrong auto-matches** | **0** | **0** |
| Fuzzy pairs found | 862 | 320 |
| **Fuzzy pairs correct** | **862 / 862** | **320 / 320** |
| True pairs left unpaired (5¢ fees score 0.94, under 0.95) | 206 | 22 |
| Incident sent to the AI | none (same as before) | PROC_B −3¢ × 222 (same as before) |

- Auto-match counts are **identical** to the original matcher: nothing is resolved without an approved rule.
- The self-improving loop is **preserved**: the PROC_B 3¢ incident still reaches the agents.
- **1,182 open cases** now arrive at the human queue with a correct, explained partner attached.
- Loosening the policy to 0.90 / 0.05 pairs all 1,410 fee cases, still with zero wrong pairs: a change to propose and approve, not to hard-code.

---

## 10. Where fuzzy adds new evidence

On this dataset every messy reference can still be cleaned, so incident evidence still comes from the original matcher.
Fuzzy adds evidence the matcher **could not produce** when references are broken or shared:

```text
12 PROC_C records, each sharing its reference with another customer's settlement
(sequential references need a 0.05 margin: neighbours score about 0.88)
   Original matcher → AMBIGUOUS, no evidence → AI sees nothing
   With cascade     → fuzzy keeps the same-customer partner (3¢ short)
                    → incident: PROC_C −3¢ × 12, evidence_sources = fuzzy: 12
```

This is the situation real bank statements, UPI QR payments, and B2B transfers create every day.

## 11. What changed in the code

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `app/tools/match_cascade.py` | **New.** The five steps and `run_cascade`, all reading the active policy |
| `app/tools/business.py` | `expected_settlement_amounts`: after-fee amounts shared by the rule step and fuzzy |
| `app/schemas.py` | `MatchSuggestion`: strict format for AI suggestions |
| `app/prompts.py` | `CASE_REVIEW` prompt for the AI reviewer |
| `app/tools/analytics.py` | Incidents record `evidence_sources` (rule or fuzzy) |
| `app/tools/matcher.py` | `BREAK_REASONS` shared constant (no behaviour change) |
| `tests/test_match_cascade.py` | **New.** 11 tests, all passing |

The original `match()` is untouched in behaviour and remains the only matcher the **backtester** uses,
so policy evaluation stays fast, deterministic, and free of AI calls.
